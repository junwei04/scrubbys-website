Formaldehyde After Renovation: Why the New Home Smell Isn't Harmless

By Scrubbys SG · Updated 7 August 2026

You collect the keys to your freshly renovated home, open the door, and there it is: that sharp new smell. Most people take it as the smell of a job well done. It is worth knowing what you are actually breathing, because that smell is largely formaldehyde and other volatile organic compounds, and it is not as harmless as it seems.

Where It Comes From

Formaldehyde is used in the adhesives that bind engineered wood, laminates and plywood, the very materials most modern carpentry is built from. New paint, varnish and some furniture release it too. In a home full of brand new built in wardrobes, kitchen cabinets and feature walls, there is a lot of freshly cut, freshly glued material all off gassing at once.

How Long It Lasts

This is the part that surprises people. Off gassing does not clear in a few days. It can continue for months, and in a warm, humid climate like Singapore the process is accelerated, because heat and humidity draw the compounds out of the materials faster. That means higher levels in the exact conditions most Singapore homes sit in year round.

Why It Matters for Your Health

At the levels found in a newly renovated space, formaldehyde and VOCs commonly cause irritation: watery eyes, a scratchy throat, headaches, and a blocked or runny nose that has nothing to do with a cold. Anyone more sensitive, young children, the elderly, and people with asthma or allergies, tends to feel it more. If a whole household starts sneezing the week they move into a new home, the air is a likely culprit.

What Actually Reduces It

  • Ventilation: open every window and run fans. It helps, but only while the air is moving, and Singapore homes are often sealed and air conditioned.
  • Time: levels fall over months as materials finish off gassing. Not ideal if you are moving in now.
  • Professional treatment: a formaldehyde treatment breaks down the compounds at the source across your carpentry and surfaces, bringing levels down quickly rather than waiting months.

Why Air Quality Readings Matter

The trouble with an invisible problem is that you cannot tell if a treatment worked. That is why our Formaldehyde Treatment includes an air quality reading before and after, so you see the actual numbers improve rather than taking anyone's word for it. We work towards a post treatment level below 0.1mg/m3 to the best of our ability, though readings naturally fluctuate with airflow, the amount of carpentry, and when the fabrication was done.

The Right Time to Treat

Treatment can only be done once your renovation is fully completed and cleaning has been done, so the surfaces are clear. That is why most customers book it together with a Post Renovation Cleaning: the clean removes the construction dust, then the treatment clears the air, all before your furniture and family move in.
